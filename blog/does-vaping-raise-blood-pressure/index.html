Does Vaping or Nicotine Raise Blood Pressure?

November 25, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida
Table of Contents

  2. Is vaping different from cigarettes, pouches, gum or patches?
  3. Does vaping cause high blood pressure in the long run?
  4. What happens to blood pressure when you quit?
  5. When should you check your blood pressure after nicotine?
  6. The bottom line

The short answer: Yes, for a short time. Nicotine from a vape, cigarette or pouch raises blood pressure and heart rate within minutes. In a 2018 randomized study, systolic pressure stayed up for about 45 minutes after vaping nicotine. Whether vaping alone causes lasting high blood pressure is still unclear, according to a 2026 meta-analysis.

How much does nicotine raise blood pressure, and for how long?

Nicotine is a stimulant. The American Heart Association lists higher heart rate and higher blood pressure among its direct effects. The rise starts within minutes of use and then fades.

The size and length of that rise depend on how much nicotine reaches the blood. Here are the numbers from the studies we could check:

  • Vaping with nicotine: In a randomized, double-blinded crossover study of 15 smokers, systolic pressure rose for about 45 minutes after vaping a nicotine liquid. Heart rate also stayed up for about 45 minutes.
  • One cigarette: In the same study, systolic pressure rose for about 15 minutes. Heart rate stayed up for about 30 minutes.
  • Nicotine-free vape: No significant change in the first hour.
  • Pooled data: A 2019 meta-analysis of 14 studies (441 people) found a single vaping session raised systolic and diastolic pressure by about 2 mmHg each. Heart rate rose by about 2 beats per minute.

A 2 mmHg average sounds small. But it is an average, and the studies used different devices and nicotine strengths. Each new session can start a new rise.

Is vaping different from cigarettes, pouches, gum or patches?

All of these deliver nicotine, so all can raise blood pressure. They differ in how fast and how much nicotine arrives. A side-by-side view:

  • Cigarettes: Fast nicotine plus smoke. In a 2024 crossover study of 15 smokers, heart rate rose about 27 beats per minute during one cigarette.
  • Vapes: Nicotine without combustion. In the 2018 study above, the rise in pressure lasted longer after vaping than after one cigarette.
  • Nicotine pouches: In the 2024 study, a 30 mg pouch raised heart rate about 25 beats per minute. A 20 mg pouch raised it about 12. The 30 mg pouch put more nicotine in the blood than the cigarette (peak 29.4 vs 15.2 ng/mL).
  • Nicotine gum and patches: These are FDA-approved quit aids. Their Drug Facts labels say to ask a doctor before use if you have high blood pressure not controlled with medication, because nicotine can increase blood pressure. In a placebo-controlled trial of 106 people with heart disease, patches did not change resting heart rate or blood pressure over 2 weeks.

The AHA draws a clear line here. It says FDA-approved nicotine replacement is designed to help you quit, while vapes and pouches are sold to keep people using nicotine.

Does vaping cause high blood pressure in the long run?

The honest answer is that nobody knows yet. Vapes have not been around long enough for strong long-term data. The 2023 AHA scientific statement on e-cigarettes says early evidence shows acute effects, mainly from nicotine, and that more research is needed.

The best current data point in two directions:

  • A 2026 cohort study followed 11,623 Hispanic and Latino adults for a median of 6 years. People who had ever vaped had a 49% higher risk of developing hypertension than people who never vaped. Their blood pressure change over time did not differ, though.
  • A 2026 meta-analysis of 10 studies found no clear link between vaping alone and new hypertension (hazard ratio 1.17, 95% confidence interval 0.83 to 1.64). Using both vapes and cigarettes was linked to higher risk (1.35). The authors rated the certainty as very low.

In these data, dual use looks like the worst of both. If you vape and still smoke, cutting out cigarettes completely matters more than cutting down.

What happens to blood pressure when you quit?

The short-term spike goes away fast. The AHA says blood pressure and heart rate begin to drop from the nicotine spike within about 20 minutes of quitting.

The bigger payoff is in heart risk, not just the number on the cuff. The CDC gives this timeline after quitting smoking:

  1. Within minutes: heart rate drops.
  2. 1 to 2 years: risk of heart attack drops sharply.
  3. 5 to 10 years: the added risk of coronary heart disease drops by half.
  4. 15 years: coronary heart disease risk is close to that of someone who never smoked.

Resting blood pressure does not always fall after quitting. In a Swedish study of 37 daily snus pouch users who stopped, home systolic pressure was 3.7 mmHg higher at 12 weeks. Body weight rose 1.8 kg in the same period. In contrast, the 2019 meta-analysis found that smokers who switched to vaping had systolic pressure about 7 mmHg lower over time. Watch your weight and keep checking your numbers after you quit.

If your pressure stays high despite treatment, nicotine is one item on the list of causes of resistant hypertension worth raising with your doctor. Many people vape to cope with stress. If that is you, anxiety.md covers other ways to manage it.

When should you check your blood pressure after nicotine?

The CDC says a reading may be higher if you smoked, had caffeine or alcohol, or exercised within 30 minutes. The 2018 vaping study saw raised pressure for about 45 minutes, so 30 minutes may not be enough after a vape.

Use this checklist for each home reading:

  1. Note the time of your last vape, cigarette, pouch or piece of gum.
  2. Wait at least 30 minutes, and longer after a vape or a strong pouch.
  3. Empty your bladder and sit with your back supported for 5 minutes.
  4. Keep feet flat, legs uncrossed, and the cuff on your bare upper arm at chest height.
  5. Do not talk during the reading.
  6. Write "nicotine at [time]" next to the result so your clinician can read the pattern.

Worked example (hypothetical): Sam vapes at 7:00 a.m. and checks at 7:10 a.m. That reading falls inside the window where the 2018 study saw pressure still rising, so it mostly measures the nicotine spike. Sam checks again at 8:00 a.m., with no nicotine since 7:00. The second reading is a fairer picture of Sam's baseline. Sam logs both, each labeled with the nicotine time, so the doctor sees the full pattern.

A patch gives a steadier nicotine level, so the time of day matters less. Note it in your log anyway. For more on cuff fit and timing, see our guide to home blood pressure monitoring.

The bottom line

Nicotine raises blood pressure and heart rate every time you use it, whatever the delivery method. After a vape, the rise can last about 45 minutes. Long-term data on vaping alone are still thin, but using both vapes and cigarettes is linked to higher hypertension risk. Quitting all nicotine brings fast and long-lasting heart benefits. Time your home readings around nicotine so your log shows your true baseline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a nicotine-free vape raise blood pressure?

In a small randomized crossover study, vaping a nicotine-free liquid did not change blood pressure in a significant way during the first hour. The same e-cigarette with nicotine raised systolic pressure for about 45 minutes. Nicotine seems to drive most of the short-term rise.

Is it safe to use a nicotine patch if I have high blood pressure?

The FDA Drug Facts label on nicotine patches and gum says to ask a doctor before use if your high blood pressure is not controlled with medication, because nicotine can increase blood pressure. In a 2-week placebo-controlled trial in 106 people with heart disease, patches did not change resting heart rate or blood pressure. Ask your clinician which quit aid suits you.

How long should I wait after vaping before I take my blood pressure?

The CDC says smoking within 30 minutes of a reading can make it higher. In one small study, the rise after vaping nicotine lasted about 45 minutes, so 30 minutes may not be enough after a vape. Writing down when you last used nicotine makes each reading easier to interpret.

Do nicotine pouches raise your heart rate as much as a cigarette?

A 2024 crossover study in 15 smokers found heart rate rose about 27 beats per minute with a cigarette and about 25 with a 30 mg pouch. A 20 mg pouch raised it about 12 beats per minute. The 30 mg pouch delivered more nicotine into the blood than the cigarette did.

Will my blood pressure go down right away if I stop vaping?

The American Heart Association says blood pressure and heart rate begin to drop from the nicotine spike within about 20 minutes of quitting. Longer-term changes are less clear. One Swedish study found home systolic pressure was 3.7 mmHg higher 12 weeks after people stopped using snus pouches, along with some weight gain.
